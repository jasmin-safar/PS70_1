<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"></p> <h3>Final Project: Integration Week</h3> <p>This week, I made progress on various aspects regarding my final project.</p> <p>First, I was thinking of a different way to control the flower movement. Initially, I had incorporated a button into my breadboard. However, I decided to remove the button. At first, I was thinking of creating a mechanism that allows you to touch the flower and have it open, so I tried to incorporate a copper touchpad on the petal. However, aesthetically, this did not match my vision and was also a little harder to operate. So I decided to switch it out and instead change the code so that the flower is now controlled through my phone over Wi-Fi by connecting to the ESP32’s IP address. Below you can see what the display looks like.</p> <img src="phonecontrol.jpg" alt="Phone control for flower movement" style="width:100%; max-width:600px;"> <p>In addition, I decided to incorporate three flowers, which is why I included two additional servo motors into my circuit. I now have three motors and need to add the flower mechanisms to the additional two servos that I added.</p> <p>Lastly, I went to a fabric shop to try to find some fabric for the dress that I am going to sew.</p> <p>This week, I also spent a lot of time trying to understand KiCad since I had never used it before. I designed the PCB on KiCad in order to mill it, miniaturize my circuit, and get rid of the breadboard.</p> <img src="kicad1.png" alt="KiCad PCB design screenshot 1" style="width:100%; max-width:600px;"> <img src="kicad2.png" alt="KiCad PCB design screenshot 2" style="width:100%; max-width:600px;"> <p>In terms of next steps, I will duplicate the flower mechanism that I already created twice so that I have three flowers in total. I might vary the size or colors of the flowers when they open to make the design a little more interesting. I will also need to sew the dress to incorporate all the flowers into it. I am looking forward to the next steps and am really excited to see the end result. Overall, I feel good about the progress and have learned a lot about KiCad, which was probably one of the biggest takeaways this week.</p> <h1 style="font-size: 60px; text-align: center; margin-top: 40px; margin-bottom: 40px;"> Final Project </h1> <video controls width="500"> <source src="finalvid.mp4" type="video/mp4"> </video> <p> For my final project, after having completed the work for integration week, I realized that there was still a lot left to do. I had to sew my dress, so I went to Dorchester to buy fabric from Sewfisticated. I bought some new fabric there and then went ahead and cut pieces for my skirt and top. I decided to make two separate pieces so that they could be mixed and matched when worn together. </p> <img src="1fabric.jpeg" width="350"> <p> I spent a lot of time on the sewing machine since this was one of the first major sewing projects I had worked on. I also did not use any patterns for either the skirt or the top, so I relied completely on my own measurements and a lot of creativity to put together a skirt and top that looked good. It also took me a really long time because I bought six yards of fabric in order to make the skirt very poofy. While sewing, especially when ruffling certain parts of the skirt and the sleeves, the thread would often break because the fabric sections were so long. </p> <img src="2sewing.jpeg" width="350"> <p> To create the ruffles, I had to sew using the largest stitch width my sewing machine allowed, which in this case was 5. Then I pulled the ends of the thread to gather and ruffle the fabric together. As you can imagine, doing this for six yards of fabric was very difficult and time consuming. </p> <img src="3sewing.jpeg" width="350"> <p> In addition, I decided to add pockets to the skirt. First of all, many skirts do not have pockets, which I personally do not like. Second, and most importantly, I wanted a place to house my PCB and battery. I thought the pockets would be a great way to hide all of the wiring and electronics. Below, you can see how the pockets looked when they were cut but not sewn yet. </p> <img src="4sewing.jpeg" width="350"> <p> In addition, I also had to create holes in the fabric to attach the 3D printed gears. My 3D printed gears have stands on them, as you can see in some of the images further below. However, this turned out to be more difficult than I had anticipated because I could not simply cut holes into the fabric. The fabric would fray and the threads would continue unraveling, which would eventually destroy the skirt. </p> <p> Because of this, I had to clean up each opening and sew a cross stitch around every hole. This turned out to be very difficult because the holes were extremely small. Below, you can see an image of one flower that is finished in the middle and two flowers that do not yet have the attached petals. For the CAD model of the 3D printed gears, please refer to Week 3, the 3D printing week, where I uploaded my CAD model. <p> This part of the project took a lot of time because I had to figure out a way to make all the gears move at the same time so that all the petals would open together. In addition, I also had to think of a way to attach the structure to the dress itself. Without the stands, and with only the ring and gears, the fabric would have prevented the gears from moving properly and blocked the movement entirely. The stands therefore became a very effective way to integrate the mechanism directly into the dress. </p> </p> <img src="flowers7.jpeg" width="350"> <p> The gears themselves are quarter gears with holes created in the center so that they can rotate around the ring. I had to be extremely precise with the sizing of both the ring and the gear holes because the gears needed to move smoothly around the ring, but not so loosely that they would slide around out of position. Each petal was supposed to remain more or less in its own designated position. </p> <p> The shaft in the middle creates all of the movement, so I attached a wire to the bottom of it. When the wire is pulled upward, the shaft moves upward as well, causing the gears to move along the shaft and the petals to fold downward, which opens the flower. </p> <p> I also included holes in each gear so that the wire could fit through them, and then shaped the petals out of the wires themselves. In addition, I added hot glue to each wire-to-gear connection to make sure the petals were securely attached and would not fall apart. </p> <p> Initially, I tried soldering the petal wires together. However, the wires were very thin and delicate, which made the soldered connections weak and unreliable. Instead, I decided to bind the wires together manually. Using wires for the petals turned out to be very useful because it allowed me to remain flexible and continue changing the petal shapes even after adding the petal paper on top. </p> <p> To create the petals, I took some inspiration from Pinterest and found a lot of pictures of real flowers and paper flowers to coordinate the colors. I really wanted to go with a design that allowed me to incorporate not only different flower sizes, since I made a small, medium, and larger sized one, but also different colors that really fit well together. </p> <img src="flowers8.jpg" width="350"> <p> I used acrylic paint to create this effect. This took a lot of different layers of paint. As you can see, the crepe paper below already has some structure to it. However, I also worked with water and scrunching the material together to create a more realistic looking petal effect. </p> <img src="flowerspaint9.jpeg" width="350"> <p> Below, you can see all the finished flowers. On the very bottom right side of the image, you can also see some foam with circles painted on it, which is how I ultimately attached the flowers to the skirt. After pushing the flowers through the holes that I had sewn and cleaned up, the stands were attached into those foam circles on the other side of the skirt. I decided to make the petals white so that the white dress and white petals would create more of a surprising effect when the flowers open and reveal the colors painted with acrylic paint. </p> <img src="flowerspainted10.jpeg" width="350"> <p> Below, you can see a disassembled flower. Onto the shaft, I created another part of the flower, which I painted, cut up, and glued together to create a more realistic effect. I am really happy with how this turned out because the flowers and petals looked quite realistic and also had a nice texture. This picture also shows that assembling the shaft and the gears could sometimes be a little difficult, so whenever the flower came apart, it would take me quite a long time to put all the pieces back together again. </p> <img src="flowerdis11.jpeg" width="350"> <p> After designing my very first KiCad design for my PCB during integration week, I realized that I had to redesign it. First of all, the wire connections were too thin, and second, I did not realize at the time that I could directly add the shell inside KiCad, which also took a couple of extra steps to figure out. After designing the PCB, I mirrored the image because my wires run on the backside of the copper. </p> <img src="kikad1.jpeg" width="350"> <p> Below, you can see the milled PCB. This was actually the fifth iteration because the first few versions were not correct. One of them was not mirrored, another one did not cut the holes fully through, and there was also one where I accidentally imported an outline that did not really match the engravings in the middle, which caused everything to become misaligned. Ultimately, however, it worked out very well, and I was really happy with the result. I also learned a lot through the process of PCB milling, so I am very glad that I got to experience it at least once because I had been very eager to try it. </p> <img src="pcb12.jpg" width="350"> <p> Below, you can see that after milling the PCB, I soldered my wires and my Xiao onto the PCB and braided some of the wires together. Some of the wires had to be quite long because they needed to reach from the flower mechanisms down to the pocket. </p> <img src="pcbsoldered12.jpg" width="350"> <p> Below, you can see the two finished products. In the first video, I am wearing the skirt, and in the second one, you can see it displayed on the mannequin. I was really happy that both the skirt and the top fit me perfectly. I was also glad that because of the poofiness of the skirt, you could not really see the motors or the wiring hidden inside the dress. The mechanism also worked very well, which made me really happy. </p> <p> If I could change one thing, I would probably go back into the code and adjust the degree of movement of the servos. When the dress was laying flat on the ground, the flowers opened and closed perfectly. However, I did not fully account for the fact that once the dress was hanging vertically while being worn, the movement would naturally look different because of gravity and the weight of the petals. I think I could increase the degree of servo movement slightly so that the petals themselves would open and close with a stronger motion. </p> <p> Otherwise, I think the project turned out almost exactly how I had envisioned it. I really like how I incorporated different flower sizes and different colors throughout the design. I was also very satisfied with how the skirt and top turned out, and especially with how well I managed to hide the motors and wiring inside the dress. Even while wearing it, you cannot really feel the mechanisms, so the dress remains very comfortable. </p> <p> As I mentioned earlier, on the backside of each flower I added foam pieces where the stands of the 3D printed gears could fit into place. I then cut a hole in the middle so that the shaft and wire could pass through. Behind that, I added another thin layer of foam which I used to create a perfectly fitted housing for the motors. Because of this foam backing, you cannot really feel the motors or the wiring through the dress. </p> <p> Overall, I am really happy with how this project turned out. This course has been a wonderful experience, and I really enjoyed working on this final project. It has been a very steep learning curve, and I am glad that I was able to gain so many new experiences, especially with PCB milling, sewing, and digital fabrication techniques overall. </p> <p> In the future, I would be really interested in doing another project like this, especially at the intersection of fashion and digital fabrication. I could imagine creating another version of this dress with many smaller flowers spread across the skirt, while also experimenting with how to make the gears even smaller and more miniaturized so that mechanisms like this could potentially also fit into the top part of the dress. </p> <video controls width="400"> <source src="demome.mp4" type="video/mp4"> </video> <video controls width="400"> <source src="demo.mp4" type="video/mp4"> </video> </div>
