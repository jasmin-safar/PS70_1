<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"> </p> <h3>Week 3: Hand Tools and Fabrication</h3> <h4>Kinetic Sculpture</h4> <video width="600" controls> <source src="flower.mp4" type="video/mp4"> <source src="flower.webm" type="video/webm"> </video> <p> For the kinetic sculpture, I decided to make a flower that blooms, meaning it opens and then closes again. The inspiration for this was my love for flowers and for spring, when you see all the flowers bloom and reveal beautiful colors. </p> <p> I had to think of designing a mechanism that allows all petals to open up simultaneously. In my first step, I assembled a flower that can open if you pull down a ring, as you can see in the image below. </p> <img src="kin1.jpeg" alt="First version of kinetic flower mechanism" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;"> <p> To create this base of the blooming flower, I ripped apart some cardboard and created a very thin, flexible material. At first, I tried to laser cut my design; however, it did not turn out well, so instead I decided to use the cardboard material manually. </p> <img src="kin2.jpeg" alt="Thin flexible cardboard material" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;"> <p> Out of that cardboard, I rolled up the thin cardboard diagonally to create a tight tube, which served as the stem of the flower. Then I cut out 12 thin, long strips of cardboard from the cardboard that I had taken apart, so it was pretty flexible but still sturdy enough. </p> <p> Six of those strips I attached to the very top of the stem, meaning the tube structure that I had made before. Then I took an additional little strip of cardboard and wrapped it around the stem. I made sure not to wrap it too tightly so that it could be moved up and down around the stem. </p> <p> Then I placed the other six strips of cardboard onto that movable ring made out of cardboard. I put some hot glue on the edge of each of those six strips that I attached to the movable ring on the stem and attached the tips to the six strips that were attached to the very top of the stem. That way, I created the structure that can be seen in image number one. </p> <p> After that, I cut out some petals out of cardboard and glued them onto the top strips around the stem to create the visual effect of a flower and its petals. At this point, I had designed a flower that could be opened and closed through manual movement of the ring around the stem. If you pulled it down, it opened, and if you pushed it back up, it closed again. </p> <p> The ring is attached to six strips, which are then attached to the six top strips around the stem. When the ring moves up, the strips on the ring move up as well, and the petals close. When the ring is pulled down, the strips are also pulled down with the ring, and the petals open. </p> <p> The next step was to think about how to automate this and make it a kinetic sculpture without manually having to pull the ring. So I decided to think of a mechanism that could pull down the ring and push it back up directly. </p> <p> From our class exercise, I was inspired to use a rack and pinion design to translate circular movement into up-and-down movement. I modeled and laser-cut the pieces for the gears of my rack and pinion design. I also laser-cut a stand for the flower, glued the flower that I had made on top of it, and created a little housing structure behind that piece of cardboard to house my motor. Below you can find my CAD models for the cardboard I attached to the motor, the motor housing, and the rack and pinion gears. </p> <img src="kin9.jpeg" alt="First version of kinetic flower mechanism" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;"> <img src="kin10.jpeg" alt="First version of kinetic flower mechanism" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;"> <p> I then attached my motor to the circular gear of the rack and pinion design, and this created my kinetic sculpture, which opens and closes the flower. </p> <video controls style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;"> <source src="flower_raw.mp4" type="video/mp4"> </video> <p> For some finishing touches, I painted paper towels with acrylic paint. I added multiple layers, let them dry on my heating, and then glued those petals made out of paper towel onto the cardboard petals. I also made a leaf by painting a paper towel green, cutting it into a leaf shape, and placing it onto my heating while shaping it into a more realistic leaf form. </p> <img src="kin3.jpeg" alt="First version of kinetic flower mechanism" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;"> <img src="kin4.jpeg" alt="First version of kinetic flower mechanism" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;"> <p> The paper towel surprisingly also provided the leaves and petals with a very natural-looking structure. I also painted the stem green to create a more realistic look of a flower and finally had my kinetic sculpture: the blooming paper flower. </p> <video width="600" controls> <source src="flower.mp4" type="video/mp4"> <source src="flower.webm" type="video/webm"> </video> </div>
